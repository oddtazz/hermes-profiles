## S6. DigitalOcean, "Technical Writing Guidelines"
- URL: https://www.digitalocean.com/community/tutorials/digitalocean-s-technical-writing-guidelines
- Authors: Hazel Virdó, Brian Hogan, Caitlin Postal. Updated August 18, 2022.

Excerpts:
- Four style principles: "Comprehensive and written for all experience levels / Technically detailed and correct / Practical, useful, and self-contained / Friendly but formal".
- "We avoid words like 'simple,' 'straightforward,' 'easy,' 'simply,' 'obviously,' and 'just,' as these words make assumptions about the reader's knowledge."
- "Every command should have a detailed explanation, including options and flags as necessary. Every block of code should be followed by prose explanations that describe what it does and why it works that way."
- "Authors test their tutorials to ensure they work by following them exactly as written on fresh servers."
- "at the end of an article, the reader should have a usable environment or an example to build upon."
- "articles do not include jargon, memes, excessive slang, emoji, or jokes."
- "Unlike blog posts, we do not use the first person singular."
- Introduction must answer: what is it about; why learn it; what will the reader do or create; what will they have accomplished.
- "keep the focus on the problem the reader is solving rather than the technology."
- Prerequisites as a linked checklist; "Be specific with your prerequisites. A prerequisite like 'Familiarity with JavaScript' without a link … doesn't give your reader much context."
- Procedural structure: Title, Introduction, Prerequisites, Step 1…n, Conclusion.

## S7. Google, Technical Writing One: "Audience" and "Documents"
- URLs: https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience (updated 2025-03-28); https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/documents (updated 2025-07-07)
- "good documentation = knowledge and skills your audience needs to do a task − your audience's current knowledge and skills"
- Define audience by role and by "proximity" to the knowledge; proximity fades with time.
- "Experts often suffer from the curse of knowledge… From the novice's point of view, the curse of knowledge is a 'File not found' linker error due to a module not yet compiled."
- Prefer simple words; avoid idioms ("sticky wicket", "Bob's your uncle") for international readers and translation software.
- "A good document begins by defining its scope… A better document additionally defines its non-scope."
- "Imagine that your peers might only read the first paragraph of your document."
- "compare and contrast your ideas with concepts that your audience already understands."

## S8. Diátaxis, "Start here"
- URL: https://diataxis.fr/start-here (Daniele Procida)
- "there are fundamentally four identifiable kinds of documentation… tutorials, how-to guides, reference and explanation. Each has a different purpose, and needs to be written in a different way."
- Tutorial = "a lesson"; how-to = "addresses a real-world goal or problem" for "an already-competent user"; reference = "technical description… free of distraction and interpretation"; explanation = "context and background… helps answer the question why?" and "can contain opinions."
- "Crossing or blurring the boundaries described in the map is at the heart of a vast number of problems in documentation."
- "writers of tutorials who are anxious that their students should know things overload their tutorials with distracting and unhelpful explanation."

## S9. Smashing Magazine, "Writing A Smashing Article"
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/write-for-us (last updated 4 Jan 2021, Vitaly Friedman)
- Pitch must state: target audience and experience level; what they "will take away"; "Why are you the best person to write this article?"; a 200-300 word outline.
- "Practical advice that comes from real experience, plus well researched opinion is what we aim for."
- Rejects content marketing: "Think about what you have learned, not how you can sell to our readers."
- Case studies "need to focus on practical examples which will be useful to other people, not just a good story."
- Publishing policy: every article reviewed, needs "at least two positive reviews" (https://www.smashingmagazine.com/publishing-policy).
