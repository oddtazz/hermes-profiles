# Source dossier: essayists and reading research

Captured: 2026-10-14 via web_extract.

## S10. Paul Graham, "How to Write Usefully" (February 2020)
- URL: https://paulgraham.com/useful.html
- "Useful writing makes claims that are as strong as they can be made without becoming false."
- "Useful writing tells people something true and important that they didn't already know, and tells them as unequivocally as possible."
- The four components (correctness, importance, novelty, strength) "are like numbers you can multiply together".
- "If you don't learn anything from writing an essay, don't publish it."
- On qualification: "'I think x' is a weaker statement than simply 'x.' Which is exactly why you need 'I think.' You need it to express your degree of certainty."
- Process: "loose, then tight. I write the first draft of an essay fast… Then I spend days rewriting it."

## S11. Paul Graham, "Write Simply" (March 2021)
- URL: https://paulgraham.com/simply.html
- "I try to write using ordinary words and simple sentences."
- "The less energy they expend on your prose, the more they'll have left for your ideas."
- "a lot of your readers won't be native English speakers… you can't assume that writing about a difficult topic means you can use difficult words."
- "fancy writing doesn't just conceal ideas. It can also conceal the lack of them."

## S12. Nielsen Norman Group, "How Users Read on the Web" (Jakob Nielsen, 1997)
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
- "79 percent of our test users always scanned any new page they came across; only 16 percent read word-by-word."
- Recommends: highlighted keywords, "meaningful sub-headings (not 'clever' ones)", bulleted lists, "one idea per paragraph", inverted pyramid "starting with the conclusion", "half the word count (or less)".
- Measured usability vs promotional control: concise +58%, scannable +47%, objective language +27%, all three combined +124%.
- "Users detested 'marketese'."
- Caveat: study is from 1997 and tested a travel site, not technical articles.

## S13. Nielsen Norman Group, "F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web" (Kara Pernice, 2017)
- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/
- "First lines of text on a page receive more gazes than subsequent lines"; "First few words on the left of each line of text receive more fixations."
- Pattern "alive and well… both on desktop and on mobile." Not the only scanning pattern; "Good design can prevent F-shape scanning."

## Method note
Search queries were run for: developer blogging advice, DigitalOcean guidelines, Diátaxis, Google tech-writing course, Julia Evans writing, Smashing guidelines, Paul Graham on writing, Dan Luu on writing (no usable primary text found for Luu), NN/g reading research. Primary pages were fetched and read; secondary aggregator pages were used only to locate them. Selection bias: sources are English-language, web/blog-oriented, and mostly practitioner opinion rather than controlled studies. Only S12/S13 report measured reader behaviour.
