## S1. "How to Write Blog Posts that Developers Read"
- URL: https://refactoringenglish.com/chapters/write-blog-posts-developers-read/
- Author: Michael Lynch. Book excerpt. Author states 9 years of blogging, 300k-500k unique readers/year, 30+ Hacker News front pages.

Excerpts:
- "The biggest mistake software bloggers make is meandering."
- "When the reader arrives, they're trying to answer two questions as quickly as possible: 1. Did the author write this article for someone like me? 2. How will I benefit from reading it? Give yourself the title plus your first three sentences to answer both questions."
- Benefits listed: "A technique the reader can apply…", "A clear explanation of a concept…", "An insight that gives the reader a better understanding…", "A story or rant that resonates with the reader."
- Example opener: "if got, want: A Simple Way to Write Better Go Tests. There's an excellent Go testing pattern that too few people know. I can teach it to you in 30 seconds."
- "Think one degree bigger": "Often, the change is just an extra sentence or two early in the article to introduce a concept or replace jargon with more accessible terms." Siacoin post example: rewritten for non-crypto readers, reached HN front page.
- "Plan the route to your readers": "Before you begin writing, think through how readers will find your post." Beginner Python tutorial example: cannot rank on Google, /r/python bans links, HN dislikes tutorials.
- "The biggest bang-for-your-buck change you can make to a blog post is adding pictures." Screenshots, graphs, diagrams. "AI-generated images … are better than nothing, but they're worse than anything else, including terrible MS Paint drawings."
- "Many readers skim an article first to decide if it's worth reading… If the reader only saw your headings and images, would it pique their interest?" Provides a "skimmify" bookmarklet that strips p/code/ul/ol/pre/blockquote.
- (para) Contrast of two of his posts: vague headings attracted few readers; "I Regret My $46k Website Redesign" with story-bearing headings drew 150k unique readers in its first week.

## S2. "Anti-Patterns in Software Blogging"
- URL: https://refactoringenglish.com/blog/anti-patterns-software-blogging
- Author: Michael Lynch.

Excerpts:
- "The most common mistake in software blogging, by far, is meandering."
- "Preamble still counts as meandering": subtitle, bio, image, famous quote "count against your 'inspire the reader to keep reading' budget."
- "The reader knows everything I know except this one thing": bad example introduces Docker via "cgroups" and "*BSD jails". Advice: "Write a list of terms they would recognize and terms they would not."
- "Overreliance on links": "Your target reader should be able to enjoy and understand your article from start to finish without clicking any links." Links should be "a bonus rather than a prerequisite."
- "The sequel injection bug": "most readers have not read part one."
- "Excessive formality": "Just write the way you talk." Bad: "Several static analysis tools were utilized by my teammates and myself throughout the duration of this project's lifetime." Good: "We tried a few static analyzers on this project."
- "With so many developers delegating their writing to AI, software blogging is becoming bland and homogenous. Readers are hungry for writing with personality."
- Rendering basics: no horizontal overflow on mobile ("25% of you are reading this page on your phones"; 35% on his blog), no low-contrast text.
